01

First contact

Before any agreement is signed

The moment a prospect becomes specific — you're discussing a property or writing terms with them — this form is due, client or not.

525

Duties Owed by a Nevada Real Estate Licensee

Hard deadline

WhyIt puts in writing, before anyone says something they'd only want their own agent to hear, whether you're their advocate or just facilitating the deal.

Give to: every party — clients and unrepresented customers.

When: NRED's own regulation is explicit — "as soon as practicable but no later than the date and time on which any written document is signed," which includes a listing agreement, purchase agreement, lease, property management agreement, letter of intent, or buyer brokerage agreement.

One signed copy per transaction file is enough — the listing side keeps the seller's, the buyer side keeps the buyer's. Get a signature acknowledging receipt; if they refuse to sign, note the refusal and date rather than skipping the form.

02

Entering the brokerage agreement

Listing or buyer-rep signing

The agreement itself is the main event here — Consent to Act only triggers if you or your brokerage will represent both sides of the same deal.

GLVAR

Buyer Brokerage Representation Agreement

Sign before showing property

WhyWithout it you're working unpaid and unprotected — it locks in that the buyer owes you a fee for the work you're about to do, so you don't tour houses for months and lose the commission to a procuring-cause dispute.

Give to: buyer clients — this is the actual engagement contract (exclusive or non-exclusive), not a NRED disclosure.

When: before you're acting on the buyer's behalf under a fee agreement — i.e. at or before the first showing.

Confirmed current as of a fresh Transaction Desk pull: still Revised 10.24 — no newer version was issued for AB 258 (written brokerage agreements mandatory as of Oct 2025). Not hosted here, since it's a copyrighted GLVAR member form — pull it fresh from Transaction Desk rather than an old saved copy.

524

Consent to Act (dual / multiple representation)

Before you act for both sides

WhyRepresenting both sides is an inherent conflict of interest — this is the written permission to let you continue anyway, obtained before it can taint the negotiation.

Give to: both parties, only when one brokerage represents buyer and seller (or two clients on the same deal).

When: signed by everyone before you write or present an offer in that dual capacity.

NRS 645.252 Open Form 524 ↗
525A

Supplemental List of Licensees

Attachment

WhySame protection as Form 525, just scaled — every extra licensee touching the file needs to be on the record as covered by that same duties disclosure.

Use when: more than two licensees at your brokerage are party to the same Duties Owed disclosure — e.g. a team transaction.

When: filed alongside Form 525, same timing.

03

Under contract

10 days before closing

The seller-side disclosure clock starts running the moment you're in escrow.

547

Seller's Real Property Disclosure

10-day deadline

WhyIt shifts liability. A seller who discloses a known defect in writing generally can't be sued over it later; one who stays quiet — or lets you stay quiet for them — can be.

Seller completes → serves on buyer or buyer's agent.

When: at least 10 days before conveyance (close of escrow).

If a defect turns up — or worsens — after the form is served but before closing, disclose it in writing as soon as practicable, no later than conveyance. Deliver it late or not at all, and the buyer generally gets a right to rescind.

NRS 113.130 · NRS 113.150 Open Form 547 ↗

Situational — only if the property triggers it

These aren't universal, but each one is easy to forget precisely because it's conditional. Check every listing against this list at intake.

551

Open Range Disclosure

If adjacent to open range

WhyBuyers unfamiliar with rural Nevada can be blindsided by roaming livestock wandering onto their new property — this forces the conversation before they own the problem.

Trigger: property borders or is near unfenced open range land (common in rural NV).

When: at or before the purchase agreement is signed.

610

Manufactured Home Disclosure

If selling a manufactured home

WhyManufactured homes carry different title, financing, and inspection rules than site-built homes — this flags that distinction before the buyer assumes it's a conventional purchase.

Trigger: the property is a manufactured or mobile home.

When: at or before the offer is written.

Federal

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure + EPA pamphlet

If built before 1978

WhyFederal law, not optional — skip it and both you and the seller can be held personally liable, including treble damages, whether or not lead paint actually turns out to be present.

Trigger: any residential property built before 1978 — this is federal law, not a NRED form, so it's easy to miss on this state list.

When: before the buyer becomes contractually obligated. Buyer gets a 10-day window to test for lead paint unless waived in writing.

HOA

Common-Interest Community Resale Package

If in an HOA / CIC

WhyThe buyer is inheriting the HOA's rules, debts, and pending lawsuits sight-unseen without it — and receiving it starts their statutory window to walk away if what's inside changes their mind.

Trigger: property sits in an HOA or common-interest community. Order it the day you go under contract — it's usually the longest pole in the tent.

When: association must furnish it within 10 days of a written request; the package stays valid 90 days. Buyer typically has 5 days to cancel after receiving it.

NRS 116.4109 · NRS 116.41095 Not a static form — ordered directly from the HOA or its management company.
04

At or after closing

Owner-initiated, not deal-blocking

Not disclosures to the other party — good post-closing value-adds for your client.

654

Declaration of Homestead

Optional, buyer's own protection

WhyShields a chunk of home equity from most creditors — a five-minute filing that's easy to forget once the closing excitement is over.

Filed by: the new owner, with the County Recorder, after taking title.

When: anytime after closing — worth mentioning to every buyer as a follow-up.

Open Form 654 ↗
657

Restrictive Covenant Modification

If old deed has unlawful language

WhyStrikes now-illegal discriminatory language out of the chain of title — matters for client comfort and, at some lenders/title companies, is required before a future resale will clear.

Trigger: title work surfaces discriminatory covenant language in an older deed or CC&Rs.

When: filed with the County Recorder as needed — not on a transaction clock.
